1. WHAT XCOM-XT IS
------------------
XCOM-XT is a new engine, written from scratch, that plays X-COM: UFO
Defense (MicroProse, 1994) on PCs far older than the original needs. The
target is a 10 MHz turbo XT (8088 or NEC V20, 640 KB, EGA) with a hard
disk; the same game also runs in CGA and Hercules, and as a 256-colour VGA
version on a 286 or better.

The rules, numbers and screens follow the DOS game (with OpenXcom's
documented rules as the reference for the details). What was cut to fit
an XT is mostly animation and graphics, not game mechanics: soldiers jump
from tile to tile instead of sliding, explosions are one picture, and the
world is a flat map instead of a rotating globe. Section 12 lists the
differences.

There is no game data in the download. XCOMCONV.EXE, a Windows program,
turns your own copy of the game into the packs the DOS program reads.


2. STATUS OF THIS ALPHA
-----------------------
Alpha 0.5.1 is the first public build. The whole campaign can be played
from the first base to Cydonia, but expect bugs, and expect that saved
games and packs from this version will not work with later ones.

Works:
 - The full campaign: geoscape with time, alien missions of every DOS
   type (research, harvest, abduction, infiltration, alien base, terror,
   retaliation, supply), UFOs on their original flight paths, radar,
   interception and air combat, 16 funding countries with monthly
   reports, pacts, losing the game after two bad months.
 - Up to 8 bases on DOS's 6x6 grid, with facilities, staff, stores,
   research, manufacture, purchase, sell, transfers, hangars, psi
   training, alien containment and base defence against a battleship.
 - All three transports (Skyranger, Lightning, Avenger) with their own
   maps and seats, the five tanks and hovertanks (HWPs), interceptors and
   their weapons, Firestorm, Lightning, Avenger.
 - Battles on all 6 terrains with all 8 UFO types, terror sites in cities
   with civilians, alien bases, both stages of Cydonia; 5 alien races and
   their terror units; fog of war, time units, reaction fire, morale,
   panic, psionics, stun and live captures, smoke, fire, explosions that
   destroy terrain, night battles with flares.
 - Research tree, UFOpaedia with the game's own texts and pictures,
   soldier stats and experience, the debriefing and the score.
 - 10 save slots, including saving in the middle of a battle.
 - The intro movie and the end slides; the original AdLib music.
 - Quick battles (the BATTLE option), like OpenXcom's New Battle.

Partly done or limited:
 - Sound Blaster: music and sound effects play on its FM chip (as on an
   AdLib). The original digitised effects play only when there is memory
   outside the 640 KB to hold them (an upper memory block of 8 KB or more,
   or XMS on a 286 or better). Without it you get FM or PC speaker
   versions of the effects. The intro has no digitised effects at all.
 - The VGA version is newer than the others and has had less play.
 - Some rare limits on the biggest battles of a plain 640 KB XT (a tank
   may have to stay in the craft, floor items drawn as marks): see
   section 8. Upper memory or EMS/XMS removes them.

Not tested:
 - Hercules has only been run in an emulator, never on a real card or a
   real mono monitor.
 - XCOMV20.EXE has never run on a real NEC V20 (the emulators' V20 is not
   exact). XCOMXT.EXE is the safe choice on a V20.
 - Most speed and memory figures in this manual come from the 86Box
   emulator. Real machines can be faster or slower, especially disks,
   upper memory on 8-bit cards and 16-bit Sound Blasters in an XT.
 - DOSBox and other emulators have had little or no testing.


3. CHOOSING YOUR HARDWARE: WHAT TO EXPECT AT EACH LEVEL
-------------------------------------------------------
All levels play the same game with the same rules. What changes is speed,
colours and how many of the memory compromises you meet.

Level 1 - plain XT: 8088 or V20 at 8-10 MHz, 640 KB, hard disk.
  Video: EGA (with 256 KB) looks best; CGA (4 colours) and Hercules
  (greys) work. Free memory about 560-570 KB (DOS, a mouse driver and
  nothing else). Program: XCOMXT.EXE.
  Expect: alien turns of 5-20 seconds on a busy map, up to about 30 s on
  a terror site with civilians; a full battle redraw about 0.6 s on EGA.
  Walking shows 2 leg frames. In the biggest battles (the jungle
  battleship, terror sites with tanks) some pictures are shared or read
  from disk as drawn (section 8). Night battles use one dark palette.

Level 2 - XT with upper memory and/or an EMS board.
  Upper memory blocks (UMBs) from a memory board and its UMB driver, or
  DOS 5+ with DOS=UMB on a machine that supports it. EMS from a LIM 3.2 or
  4.0 board (for example a Lo-tech EMS board) and its driver.
  Expect, with a UMB of 44 KB or more: none of the big-battle compromises
  showed in testing, less code loading from disk, Sound Blaster
  digitised effects, 4 leg frames when walking, and on EGA two levels of
  night light. With EMS (or XMS): up to 2 MB of what the game reads stays
  in memory, so screens and battle rounds you have seen before read
  almost nothing from disk (campaign trips to a battle and back roughly
  twice as fast in the reading part), and battle views redraw about 3 to
  4 times faster.

Level 3 - 286 (or 386, 486) with VGA and HIMEM.SYS.
  Program: XCOM286.EXE. With a VGA card, DOS 5 or later, HIMEM.SYS and
  256 KB or more of free XMS, the game picks the 256-colour VGA version
  by itself. Exact sight and AI (section 12) are on by default here.
  Expect on an 8 MHz 286: a full battle redraw about 0.2-0.3 s, the first
  alien turn of a jungle battle about 20 s with exact mode on. Sound
  Blaster digitised effects come from XMS. DOS=HIGH helps memory.
  The same PC can also play the EGA version (XCOM286 EGA).

Faster PCs: 386 and up run XCOM286.EXE and everything is quicker. Speeds
in the game options keep DOS's timing, so the game does not race.


4. MAKING THE DATA: XCOMCONV
----------------------------
XCOMCONV.EXE runs on 64-bit Windows and needs nothing else installed. It
reads the original game's files and writes the packs XCOM-XT uses.

  XCOMCONV [game folder] [-o output folder] [options]

 - With no folder it looks for Steam and GOG installs, then asks. The
   folder is the one that holds GEODATA, GEOGRAPH, MAPS, ROUTES, SOUND,
   TERRAIN, UFOGRAPH, UFOINTRO and UNITS; a Steam or GOG install folder
   around it also works (it looks one or two levels down). A copy of the
   CD's game folder works too.
 - It checks that the data is the DOS 1.4 version and stops with a clear
   message if files are missing or the version is wrong. Other versions
   (the Windows edition, Terror from the Deep, mods) are not supported.
 - Output: a PACKS folder next to XCOMCONV.EXE (or the -o folder). Re-run
   it any time: it overwrites the old packs.
 - It takes about 2 minutes on a recent PC (longer on an old one) and
   runs the EGA, CGA and VGA conversions side by side.

Options:
  -o DIR             write the packs to DIR instead of PACKS
  --skip-check       try data that is not exactly DOS 1.4 (no guarantee)
  --sequential       one video mode at a time (less memory, slower)
  --openxcom DIR     an OpenXcom installation (or its en-US.yml): texts the
                     game files lack come from there instead of our own
  --prefer-openxcom  with --openxcom: take every UFOpaedia, briefing and
                     slide text from OpenXcom

What it writes (about 42 MB in all):
  PACKS\        PACKS.VER (the format version), MUSIC.PAK, SOUNDS.PAK,
                NAMES.PAK (soldier names), UFOPAED.PAK (UFOpaedia texts)
  PACKS\EGA\    15 MB. EGA pictures: missions, races, armour, tanks, the
                world map, screens, intro (*E.PAK), and the extra walking
                frames (*EW.PAK) used when there is upper or extended memory
  PACKS\CGA\    9 MB. The same in CGA colours (*C.PAK, *CW.PAK); Hercules
                uses these too
  PACKS\VGA\    18 MB. The 256-colour version (*V.PAK)

No Windows? The converter is a Python program: with Python 3.12 and PyYAML
installed, run  python tools/xcomconv.py <game folder>  from the SOURCE
folder; it does the same on Linux or macOS.

The packs come from your copy of the game. Keep them for yourself; do not
upload or share them.


5. INSTALLING ON THE DOS PC
---------------------------
 1. Make a folder on the hard disk (C:\XCOMXT, for example).
 2. Copy XCOMXT.EXE, XCOMV20.EXE, XCOM286.EXE (or just the one you use).
 3. Copy the files in PACKS itself: PACKS.VER, MUSIC.PAK, SOUNDS.PAK,
    NAMES.PAK, UFOPAED.PAK.
 4. Copy the mode folder you need as a subfolder: EGA, CGA or VGA. Any
    one folder is enough for its mode. Several can sit side by side.
 5. Run the program from that folder.

The game does not fit a floppy: a hard disk (or CF/SD adapter) is
required. Saved games are written to the game folder (XCOMXT0.SAV to
XCOMXT9.SAV), so it must be writable.

Packs placed directly in the game folder (all in one heap) also work: the
game looks in its mode's folder first, then in its own folder.

Recommended DOS set-up: as much free conventional memory as you can get.
Check with MEM ("Largest executable program size").
 - XT: DOS, the mouse driver and nothing else. If you have a UMB driver,
   load the mouse driver (and DOS buffers, if your DOS can) high. If you
   have an EMS board, load its driver; the game finds it.
 - 286 and up: DEVICE=HIMEM.SYS and DOS=HIGH in CONFIG.SYS; DOS=HIGH,UMB
   with EMM386 on a 386. Leave XMS free for the game (the VGA version
   needs 256 KB of it).


6. THE THREE PROGRAMS
---------------------
All three play the same game in every video mode with the same packs and
saves; they are built for different processors.

  XCOMXT.EXE   8086 code. Runs on everything: 8088, 8086, V20, V30, 286
               and up. If another one misbehaves, use this one.
  XCOMV20.EXE  80186 code: NEC V20/V30, 80186/188 and up. A little faster
               than XCOMXT.EXE. Never tested on a real V20: if it fails
               there, please report it and use XCOMXT.EXE.
  XCOM286.EXE  80286 code: a 286 or better only, never on an XT. About 5 %
               faster than XCOMXT.EXE in alien turns.

The examples in this manual say XCOMXT; use the name of your program.


7. VIDEO MODES
--------------
With no option the game picks a mode at the start:
  1. VGA, when the PC has a VGA card, a 286 or better, the VGA folder and
     256 KB of free XMS. (An XT with an 8-bit VGA card plays in EGA.)
  2. EGA, when it has an EGA or VGA card and the EGA folder.
  3. Hercules (a mono card only) or CGA, with the CGA folder.
XCOMXT LOG LOG.TXT writes the choice and the reason ("mode: ...").

Force a mode with EGA, CGA, HERC or VGA on the command line. If its card or
its folder is missing the game says so and stops; it does not fall back.

 EGA   320x200 in 16 colours, the original pictures reduced to EGA colours.
       Needs a card with 256 KB (most EGA cards; any VGA card).
 CGA   320x200 in 4 colours, dithered. Readable but crowded; on a VGA
       card's CGA emulation it can look worse than on a real CGA monitor.
 HERC  Hercules (HGC with 64 KB, both pages): the CGA picture shown in
       greys at 640x200 in the middle of the screen. Two of the four greys
       look alike, so some highlights show as plain white. Not tested on a
       real card. HERC says why when it cannot start (a colour card
       present, an MDA, a card without the second page).
 VGA   320x200 in 256 colours, the original look. A 286 or better, a VGA
       card, DOS 5+, HIMEM.SYS with 256 KB+ of free XMS, a mouse.


8. MEMORY: WHAT THE GAME DOES WITH WHAT YOU HAVE
------------------------------------------------
Rule one: the game itself comes first. Music, sound and speed-ups only use
memory the battle does not need. Nothing about the rules changes with
memory; only how things are drawn, how often the disk is read, and in
rare cases what fits into one battle.

Conventional memory (what MEM calls "Largest executable program size"):
  568 KB or more  everything: every unit with its own picture in every
                  battle, on a plain 640 KB machine.
  about 540-568   the game plays; in the biggest battles some vehicles or
                  armour share a picture (a hovertank may look like a
                  tank) and the battle's title line says "Low memory".
  below 540       wall and object pictures are read from disk as they are
                  drawn: slower, but the battle happens. The game has been
                  run down to 490 KB (not in Hercules). Less is untested.

On a plain 640 KB XT, the tightest battles (a battleship in the jungle, a
terror site, with a tank and a hovertank) can also bring:
 - a tank or hovertank that stays in the craft for that battle;
 - items on the floor shown as plain marks instead of their pictures;
 - no picture for explosions (the sound and the damage are the same);
 - no music in that battle (it plays only when 16 KB stay free);
 - city layouts with many building types coming up a little less often;
 - a pool of 96 carried items for the whole squad: a 20-soldier base
   defence or a full Avenger with heavy loadouts may leave the last
   soldiers with fewer items; 64 items on the floor at most.

Upper memory blocks (UMBs): from DOS 5 (DOS=UMB), an XMS driver that offers
UMBs, or an XT memory board's UMB driver; EMM386 on a 386. NOUMB leaves
them to sound only, for comparing.
  any size        overlay code is kept there, so less is loaded from disk
                  (32 KB already helps as much as 64 KB).
  8 KB or more    Sound Blaster digitised effects (some; 32 KB holds all
                  of a screen's music and effects, 45 KB all of them).
  44 KB or more   the battle map buffer (39 KB) moves out of the 640 KB:
                  in testing, none of the big-battle compromises above
                  showed any more. Item pools grow to 151 carried and 125
                  on the floor.
  60 KB or more   a second code area: the player's turn stays loaded.
  96 KB or more   more tables move out (19.5 KB): 255 carried and 240
                  floor items.
  about 120 KB    (an XT with RAM at D000-EFFF): no code reloads at all in
                  battle or on base screens.
With a UMB or EMS/XMS the soldiers walk with 4 leg frames instead of 2, and
on EGA the night has two light levels instead of one dark palette (DOS has
16 levels; CGA and Hercules have no night darkening).

EMS or XMS (optional, up to 2 MB used): a LIM 3.2/4.0 EMS board with its
driver, or HIMEM.SYS on a 286 or better.
 - A read cache: what the game reads from disk (packs and code) stays
   there, so screens and battles seen before read almost nothing. In the
   emulator a trip from the geoscape to a battle and back spent 16.5 s
   reading without it, 8.3 s with EMS, 10.5 s with XMS.
 - A picture store: removes the big-battle compromises above (every unit
   its own picture, no "Low memory" line).
 - A canvas for battle views: full redraws drop from about 0.6 s to about
   0.15 s (EMS) or 0.2 s (XMS) on an XT with EGA.
 - EMS costs under 1 KB of conventional memory; XMS costs 11 KB unless
   there is a UMB to hold its code.
 - EMS is used when both are present (NOEMS forces XMS); NOXMEM turns the
   cache off; XMEMKB n caps the XMS used. Memory is released on exit (a
   Ctrl-Break or crash in a battle may leave EMS pages taken until you
   reboot).
 - On a 286 without upper memory the VGA and EGA versions keep an 8 KB
   code area in conventional memory for the canvas; NOCVAREA leaves it out.


9. SOUND
--------
 - AdLib (or the FM chip of any Sound Blaster, at 388h): the original
   music, and FM versions of the sound effects. Music is skipped on a
   screen where it would leave less than 16 KB free.
 - No sound card: sound effects on the PC speaker.
 - Sound Blaster digitised effects (8-bit DMA; found through the BLASTER
   variable or at 220h/240h): played only from memory outside the 640 KB:
   a UMB of 8 KB or more (must be real memory the DMA can reach: an XT RAM
   card, or EMM386 on a 386), or XMS on a 286 or better (HIMEM.SYS). With
   neither, a Sound Blaster sounds like an AdLib, and the LOG says why
   ("sound: no digital effects: ...").
 - The game uses only Sound Blaster 1.x/2.0 commands. A Sound Blaster 16
   works in an XT's 8-bit slot only on jumpered models with 8-bit DMA;
   plug-and-play models need a 286 or better to set them up.
 - The effects are the original 23, stored smaller (4-bit), one at a time
   by priority: grainier than the original, and some events share a sound.
 - The intro movie plays with its AdLib music but without its digitised
   effects.
 - In the game, S toggles sound; the options have NOSB, NOMUSIC, NOFMSFX
   (PC speaker effects instead of FM) and NOSOUND.
 - The FM effects and the Sound Blaster on real hardware have had little
   testing.


10. LAUNCH OPTIONS
------------------
Options are words after the program name, in any order and any case, no
dashes: XCOMXT EGA NOINTRO. Options with a value take the next word:
XCOMXT LOG LOG.TXT. Unknown words are ignored.

Video
  EGA | CGA | HERC | VGA   force a video mode (section 7)

Sound
  NOSB        do not use the Sound Blaster's digitised effects
  NOMUSIC     no music
  NOFMSFX     sound effects on the PC speaker instead of the FM chip
  NOSOUND     no sound at all

Memory
  NOUMB       leave upper memory to sound only (for comparing)
  NOXMEM      do not use EMS or XMS (also rules out the VGA version)
  NOEMS       use XMS even when EMS is there
  XMEMKB n    use at most n KB of XMS for the cache
  NOCVAREA    no 8 KB canvas code area on a 286 without upper memory
              (setting XCV=OFF in the environment does the same)

Sight and AI
  EXACTAI     exact sight and alien fire lines, as DOS (default on 286+)
  NOEXACTAI   faster tile-based sight first (default below a 286)
              The later of the two on the command line wins. See
              section 12; exact mode costs an XT minutes per alien turn.

Start and diagnostics
  NOINTRO     skip the intro movie (any key or click also skips it)
  LOG file    write a diagnostic log: CPU, video mode, memory, mouse,
              sound and why each was chosen, plus battle events
  BENCH       run a fixed benchmark battle and write BENCH.TXT

Quick battles (instead of the campaign)
  BATTLE      quick random battles, one after the other
  MISSION f   a given mission pack, for example MISSION FA1E.PAK; the name
              is terrain + UFO + mode letter: terrains FA farm, FO forest,
              JU jungle, DE desert, MO mountain, PO polar; UFO 1 small
              scout, 2 medium scout, 3 large scout, 4 harvester, 5
              abductor, 6 terror ship, 7 battleship, 8 supply ship; UR0 a
              terror site in a city.
              Use the mode's letter: E (EGA), C (CGA or Hercules), V (VGA)
  RACE f      a given alien race pack: RS sectoids, RF floaters, RN
              snakemen, RM mutons, RE ethereals, RX mixed; + mode letter,
              for example RACE RSE.PAK
  NIGHT       a night battle
  ARMOUR n    the soldiers' armour, 0-3 (none, personal, power suit,
              flying suit)
  SQUAD n     n soldiers (default 8; at most the craft's seats)
  CRAFT S|L|A land in the Skyranger, Lightning or Avenger
  HWP digits  tanks along: 1 Tank/Cannon, 2 Tank/Rocket Launcher,
              3 Tank/Laser Cannon, 4 Hovertank/Plasma, 5 Hovertank/
              Launcher; HWP 14 brings a Tank/Cannon and a Hovertank/Plasma
  ALIENS n    at most n aliens
  DIFF n      difficulty 0-4 (Beginner to Superhuman)
  MONTH n     the aliens' weapons as after n months of a campaign
  SEED n      the random seed: the same number gives the same battle
  GEAR        heavy weapons, launchers, stun gear and a motion scanner
              for the first soldiers
  PSI         psi skill and Psi-Amps for the soldiers
  CRASHED     the UFO has crashed rather than landed

The program also has test and debugging options (KEYS, DEBUG, SEEALL,
SHOTLOG and others) used by the developers' scripts. They can spoil a
game; they are described in SOURCE\BUILDING.md.


11. PLAYING: KEYS AND MOUSE
---------------------------
The screens work with the mouse as in the DOS game. The keys below are
there for keyboard players and for speed.

Start menu: N new game (then a difficulty), L load, Q quit. Place the
first base with the mouse, or the arrows and Enter.

Geoscape
  mouse         click a base, craft, UFO or site for its window, as in DOS;
                right click centres the map there
  arrows        scroll the map;  + / -  zoom in / out (1:1, 2x, 4x)
  1-4           time speed;  Space  pause
  Tab           select a detected UFO, terror site or alien base;
  A             select a site only
  Enter         send the current base's ready transport to the selection
  I             launch the next armed interceptor at the selected UFO
  P             send a craft to patrol a point (finds alien bases)
  B             base screens;  R  soldiers;  F  funding;  G  graphs
  U             UFOpaedia;  S  save;  C  final assault (when researched)
  D             bring back minimised air combats
  Esc           game options: load, save, abandon game, quit
 Pop-ups (UFO detected, terror site, landing...) stop the clock: Enter
 answers yes, Esc no or OK.

Air combat
  1-5   stance: standoff, cautious, standard, aggressive, disengage
  6 / 7 weapon 1 / 2 off or on;  M  minimise;  Tab  next combat window

Base screens
  The basescape's buttons as in DOS; 1-8 or the small bases at the top
  change base. Purchase, Sell and Transfer: each row has three buttons
  each way (1, 10, all); + / - one more or less, End all, Home none, Tab
  OK. Research and Manufacture: N new project, Enter to set its staff with
  + / - (A all free ones), I for an endless production run. A production
  run can be set to sell what it makes as soon as each unit is done (an
  OpenXcom extra).

Saving
  Ten slots (keys 1-9 and 0). Click a slot to save, type a name, Enter.
  A right click on a used slot deletes it after a question (an OpenXcom
  extra). Saves made during a battle load straight back into it.

Battlescape
  mouse              click a tile to move the cursor, click again to walk;
                     the panel buttons as in DOS
  arrows, Home, End, PgUp, PgDn   move the cursor
  + / -              level up / down          Tab   next soldier
  Enter              walk to the cursor       Space turn towards the cursor
  F / A / U          snap / aimed / auto shot at the cursor
  G                  throw a grenade          R     reload (15 TU)
  K                  kneel / stand            M     medi-kit
  I                  inventory                N     motion scanner
  V                  next visible alien       C     centre on soldier
  S                  sound on / off           E     end turn
  Esc (or F1)        options: load, save, abandon, game options
  F2                 abort the mission
 A walk stops when the walker sees a new alien; Enter (or a click) again
 goes on. Hovertanks and flying suits fly: put the cursor on a higher
 level with + and walk there.


12. DIFFERENCES FROM THE DOS ORIGINAL
-------------------------------------
The rules (time units, accuracy, damage, armour, morale, research,
funding, alien missions) follow DOS. The differences are mostly in the
look:
 - 16 colours (EGA), 4 (CGA) or greys (Hercules); 256 only in the VGA
   version.
 - A flat world map with three zoom levels instead of a rotating globe.
 - Soldiers jump from tile to tile with 2 leg frames (4 with extra
   memory) instead of a smooth walk; turning jumps to the last direction.
 - Shots are a dotted line; a thrown grenade is not drawn in flight.
 - An explosion is one picture for 0.4 s instead of an animated fireball.
 - Night: one dark palette on a plain XT; two light levels on EGA with
   upper memory (DOS has 16). Who sees whom at night is as in DOS.
 - The selected soldier's arrow and the cursor do not bob or blink; UFO
   doors open at once; the view changes level only at the end of a walk.
 - "Hidden movement" is a plain window; an hourglass pointer shows when
   the game is busy for more than half a second.
 - Sound effects are smaller and grainier, one at a time (section 9).

Sight and the aliens' thinking:
 - Below a 286 the game first checks sight along a line of tiles and only
   then uses DOS's exact voxel test. The default also gives the aliens a
   slightly simpler view when they choose where to go: in tests their
   choices differed from the exact ones in about 6 % of cases. Trees and
   bushes hide a little more than in DOS. Exact mode (EXACTAI) does
   everything as DOS but costs an XT 1.5 to 2.5 minutes per alien turn;
   it is the default on a 286 or better.
 - The aliens plan their moves in smaller steps than DOS; they can seem a
   bit more random and pick worse cover.
 - Smoke cuts sight per tile rather than per voxel: very close to DOS.

Limits:
 - At most 60 units on a battle map, 26 soldiers, 8 tanks. With every
   slot used a new unit (a zombie, say) may not appear.
 - Bases hold at most 100 soldiers each and 250 in all.
 - Bodies can be carried but not thrown.
 - A tank can sometimes slip past a wall's end diagonally where DOS sends
   it round.
 - Lists of research projects, production runs and the like have size
   limits higher than normal play needs.

Additions from OpenXcom (off unless you use them): selling a production
run's output automatically, deleting saves, the Game Options' scrolling
and speed settings.


13. SPEED
---------
Measured in the 86Box emulator; real machines will differ.

 10 MHz 8088, EGA, plain 640 KB:
   full battle redraw            about 0.55-0.6 s (CGA 0.4, Hercules 0.4)
   moving the cursor one tile    about 0.04 s
   soldier walking               about 0.06 s a step
   path search (60 TU soldier)   0.2-0.3 s
   alien turn, busy map          5-20 s (terror site with civilians ~30 s)
   alien turn, exact mode        1.5-2.5 minutes
   intro movie                   about 10 frames a second from the disk
 With EMS or XMS: redraws about 0.15-0.2 s, screens seen before 5-12
 times faster to open.
 8 MHz 286, VGA: full redraw 0.2-0.3 s; first jungle alien turn about
 20 s with exact mode.

The Game Options' speed settings (1-6) are kept in real time on any CPU;
on an 8088 the drawing itself is the limit, so the fast settings feel
alike.


14. TROUBLESHOOTING AND REPORTING BUGS
--------------------------------------
"Packs are from another version: run XCOMCONV.EXE"
   The program and the packs do not match. Use the XCOMCONV.EXE from this
   same release and copy the new PACKS (all of it, the mode folders too).
The game says a mode's folder or card is missing
   You forced a mode (EGA, CGA, HERC, VGA) whose folder is not there or
   whose card was not found. Copy the folder, or let the game choose.
It does not pick VGA on my 286
   It needs a VGA card, the VGA folder, HIMEM.SYS and 256 KB of free XMS
   in one block. XCOMXT LOG LOG.TXT says which one was missing.
Battles show "Low memory" or plain marks for items
   Free more conventional memory (section 5), or add upper memory or EMS.
No digitised sound on my Sound Blaster
   Expected without a UMB or XMS (section 9). The LOG says why.
XCOMV20.EXE or XCOM286.EXE crashes or misbehaves
   Use XCOMXT.EXE, and please report it.
Old saves do not load
   Saves from other versions may not load. This is an alpha.

To report a bug: run XCOMXT LOG LOG.TXT (with your usual options), make it
happen again, and send LOG.TXT with what you did, your CPU, video card,
memory (the MEM output), sound card and the program you used. A save made
just before the problem helps a lot.
